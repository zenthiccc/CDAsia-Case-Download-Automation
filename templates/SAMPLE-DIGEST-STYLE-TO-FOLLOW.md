### Topic
**Article VI, Section 27 — Procedure in Law-Making: Passage of Bills** (Bicameral Conference Committee Powers, Enrolled Bill Doctrine, Origination Clause [Art. VI, Sec. 24], Three-Reading & No-Amendment Rule [Art. VI, Sec. 26(2)], Non-Delegation of Taxing Power / Standby Authority [Art. VI, Sec. 28(2)]).

---

### Principle of the Case
1. **Legislative Powers of the Bicameral Conference Committee & Enrolled Bill Doctrine:** The Bicameral Conference Committee possesses broad authority to reconcile and compromise conflicting provisions between House and Senate versions of a bill, including introducing new provisions or deleting common provisions, provided the modifications are *germane* to the general subject matter committed to it (revenue generation and tax reform). Under the **"Enrolled Bill Doctrine,"** the signing of a bill by the Speaker of the House and Senate President and the certification by the Secretaries of both Houses that it was passed are conclusive proof of its due enactment, and courts will not inquire into compliance with internal parliamentary rules.
2. **Delegation of Fact-Ascertainment vs. Undue Delegation of Taxing Power:** Authorizing the President, upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance, to raise the Value-Added Tax (VAT) rate from 10% to 12% upon the occurrence of specified factual macroeconomic conditions (VAT/GDP ratio > 2.8% or deficit/GDP ratio > 1.5%) does not constitute an undue delegation of legislative power. The law is complete in itself; Congress fully exercised its policy-making power to tax, delegating merely the *administrative function of ascertaining facts* upon which the mandatory enforcement of the 12% rate is contingent.

### Nature
Consolidated Petitions for *Certiorari* and *Prohibition* under Rule 65 of the Rules of Court, with urgent prayers for Temporary Restraining Orders (TRO) and/or Writs of Preliminary Injunction, challenging the constitutionality of Republic Act No. 9337 (VAT Reform Act of 2005).

### Recit Ready Summary

* **Essential Relevant Facts:** In 2005, facing a severe national fiscal crisis where 90% of revenue was consumed by debt service, Congress enacted Republic Act No. 9337 (VAT Reform Act). R.A. No. 9337 originated in the House of Representatives via House Bills Nos. 3555 and 3705, which proposed raising VAT rates and expanding its base. Upon transmittal, the Senate passed Senate Bill No. 1950, which proposed NIRC amendments not only to VAT but also to corporate income tax rates, franchise taxes, and percentage taxes. To reconcile disagreeing provisions, a Bicameral Conference Committee (BCC) was formed. The BCC introduced a "standby authority" authorizing the President, upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance, to raise VAT from 10% to 12% effective January 1, 2006 if either of two factual conditions occurred (VAT collection/GDP > 2.8% or national deficit/GDP > 1.5%). The BCC also deleted the "no pass-on provision" (which prohibited power and oil companies from passing VAT to consumers) present in both House and Senate bills, capped creditable input VAT at 70% of output tax, imposed a 60-month amortization on capital goods input VAT, and adopted Senate amendments on corporate income taxes. Both Houses ratified the BCC Report, and President Arroyo signed R.A. 9337 into law on May 24, 2005. Five consolidated petitions were filed in the Supreme Court assailing the law. On July 1, 2005, the Supreme Court issued a TRO enjoining implementation.

* **Relevant Issue (based on syllabus topic):** Whether R.A. No. 9337 is unconstitutional due to procedural defects in its passage—specifically whether the Bicameral Conference Committee exceeded its jurisdiction, violated the Origination Clause (Art. VI, Sec. 24), and violated the Three-Reading and No-Amendment Rules (Art. VI, Sec. 26)—and whether the standby authority granted to the President constitutes an undue delegation of legislative power.

* **Ruling:** **NO.** Republic Act No. 9337 is **CONSTITUTIONAL** in its entirety, and all five petitions were **DISMISSED**.

* **Key Reason/Doctrine (Law then Application):**
  * *Procedure / Bicameral Conference Committee / Origination:* Under the Enrolled Bill Doctrine, the signing and certification of a bill by legislative leaders are conclusive proof of its due enactment. The Court will not interfere with internal parliamentary rules. The Bicameral Conference Committee has broad latitude to settle legislative differences by reconciling, modifying, adding, or deleting provisions, as long as the changes are *germane* to the subject matter committed to it (revenue generation / tax reform). The Senate's inclusion of non-VAT NIRC amendments was a valid exercise of its constitutional power to "propose or concur with amendments" to a revenue bill that originated exclusively in the House.
  * *Non-Delegation of Taxing Power:* The standby authority in Sections 4, 5, and 6 of R.A. 9337 is NOT an undue delegation of legislative power under Art. VI, Sec. 28(2). Congress did not delegate the power to tax; the intent and policy to raise VAT to 12% were fully enacted by Congress itself. The President was granted no discretion—the law uses the mandatory word **"shall,"** delegating merely the *administrative function of ascertaining facts* (determining if GDP ratios are met) upon which the law's operation is contingent.

### Detailed Facts

* On August 8, 2004, Representative Eric D. Singson introduced House Bill No. 1468 in the House of Representatives.
* On January 7, 2005, House Bill No. 3555 was introduced on first reading in substitution of HB No. 1468. President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo certified HB 3555 for immediate enactment due to the country's dire fiscal crisis (where 90% of government revenue was used for debt service).
* On January 27, 2005, the House of Representatives approved HB No. 3555 on second and third reading.
* On January 27, 2005, the House of Representatives also approved House Bill No. 3705.
* Both House bills specifically targeted amendments to VAT provisions in the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC).
* Upon transmittal to the Senate, the Senate Committee on Ways and Means reported Senate Bill No. 1950.
* SB No. 1950 proposed amendments to VAT provisions as well as amendments to non-VAT provisions of the NIRC, including corporate income tax rates (raising corporate tax from 32% to 35%, then reducing to 30% in 2009), franchise taxes, percentage taxes, and allowable interest expense deductions. SB 1950 also contained a "no pass-on provision" prohibiting power generation companies from passing VAT to residential consumers.
* On March 13, 2005, the Senate passed SB No. 1950 on third reading.
* On April 13, 2005, a Bicameral Conference Committee (BCC) composed of conferees from both Houses was created to reconcile the disagreeing provisions of HB Nos. 3555/3705 and SB No. 1950.
* In its Conference Committee Report, the BCC made the following key modifications:
  * **Standby Authority:** Reconciled the Senate's proposal to retain 10% VAT with the House's proposal for 12% by creating a "standby authority" where VAT stays at 10% until January 1, 2006, when the President *shall* raise it to 12% if either VAT collection exceeds 2.8% of GDP or national deficit exceeds 1.5% of GDP upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance.
  * **Deletion of No Pass-On Provisions:** Deleted the "no pass-on" provisos found in both HB 3705 and SB 1950, allowing power and oil companies to pass VAT on to end consumers.
  * **70% Input VAT Cap:** Inserted a limitation capping creditable input tax to 70% of output VAT in any quarter.
  * **Capital Goods Amortization:** Imposed a mandatory 60-month amortization period for input VAT on capital goods acquisitions exceeding P1,000,000.
  * **Adoption of Non-VAT Amendments:** Adopted the Senate's proposed amendments to NIRC corporate income taxes, franchise taxes, and percentage taxes.
* Both the Senate and House of Representatives separately approved and ratified the Bicameral Conference Committee Report.
* On May 23, 2005, the enrolled copy of the consolidated House and Senate version was transmitted to the President, certified by Senate President Franklin M. Drilon and House Speaker Jose C. De Venecia, Jr.
* On May 24, 2005, President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo signed the bill into law as Republic Act No. 9337 (VAT Reform Act of 2005).

### Petitioner’s Arguments

#### G.R. No. 168056 (ABAKADA GURO):
1. **Undue Delegation of Power:** Sections 4, 5, and 6 violate Art. VI, Sec. 28(2) because delegating the power to fix VAT rates to the President is an abdication of Congress's exclusive power to tax and contrary to republicanism.
2. **Impairment of Executive Control:** Mandating the President to act "upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance" nullifies the President's constitutional power of control over executive subordinates.

#### G.R. No. 168207 (Sen. Pimentel et al.):
1. **Undue Delegation:** Standby authority delegates legislative taxing power.
2. **Violation of Due Process:** The 12% VAT rate contingent on macroeconomic GDP ratios violates due process (Art. III, Sec. 1) because it creates an ambiguous, fluctuating, and unfair tax burden from year to year.
3. **Violation of No-Amendment Rule:** The BCC's insertion of standby authority violates the "no-amendment rule" upon last reading of a bill laid down in Article VI, Section 26(2).

#### G.R. No. 168461 (Pilipinas Shell Dealers et al.):
1. **Arbitrary and Confiscatory:** Capping creditable input VAT at 70% of output VAT (Sec. 8) and requiring 60-month amortization on capital goods input VAT are arbitrary, oppressive, confiscatory, and violate substantive due process and equal protection.
2. **Deprivation of Property:** Input VAT is a property right that cannot be confiscated or restricted without due process.
3. **Violation of Progressive Taxation:** The 70% limit violates the constitutional principle of progressive taxation (Art. VI, Sec. 28).

#### G.R. No. 168463 (Rep. Escudero et al.):
1. **Undue Delegation:** Unconstitutional delegation of legislative power under Sections 4, 5, and 6.
2. **BCC Lack of Jurisdiction:** The BCC acted without jurisdiction in deleting the "no pass-on provisions" agreed upon in both House and Senate bills.
3. **Violation of Origination Clause:** The Senate and BCC inserted amendments to corporate income taxes, franchise taxes, and percentage taxes (Sections 27, 28, 34, 116, 117, 119, 121, 125, 148, 151, 236, 237, 288), violating the Origination Clause (Art. VI, Sec. 24) because revenue bills must originate exclusively in the House.

#### G.R. No. 168730 (Gov. Garcia):
1. **Violation of Public Purpose:** Input VAT limitations allow VAT-registered businesses to retain tax collections, violating the principle that tax revenue should be solely allocated for public expenditures.
2. **Inequitable Passing-On:** Allowing businesses to pass VAT to consumers is inequitable and violates progressive taxation (Art. VI, Sec. 28).

---

### Respondent’s Arguments

1. **Presumption of Constitutionality & Enrolled Bill Doctrine:** R.A. No. 9337 enjoys a strong presumption of validity. Citing *Tolentino v. Secretary of Finance* and *Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*, the enrolled bill signed by legislative leaders is conclusive as to due enactment. Internal parliamentary rules are beyond judicial review.
2. **Compliance with Origination Clause:** The revenue bill originated exclusively in the House (HB 3555/3705). The Senate has full constitutional power to "propose or concur with amendments," including proposing substitute versions or amending other related NIRC provisions.
3. **No Undue Delegation:** R.A. 9337 is complete in itself. Congress fixed the policy, rate (12%), and exact conditions. The President has no discretion—the law mandates "shall"—and merely executes an administrative function of fact-ascertainment based on objective GDP data.
4. **Compliance with Due Process & Equal Protection:** Input VAT is a statutory privilege created by law, not an absolute property right. The 70% cap and amortization periods are valid tax measures designed to plug tax leakages and raise urgent revenues for public survival.

### Issue/s

1. **Whether R.A. No. 9337 violates Article VI, Section 24 (Origination Clause) and Article VI, Section 26(2) (Three-Reading Rule and No-Amendment Rule) of the Constitution due to the acts of the Bicameral Conference Committee.**
2. **Whether Sections 4, 5, and 6 of R.A. No. 9337 constitute an undue delegation of legislative power to the President in violation of Article VI, Section 28(2) and the principle of republicanism.**
3. **Whether Section 8 (70% input VAT cap and 60-month amortization) and Section 12 (5% final withholding VAT) violate Article III, Section 1 (Due Process and Equal Protection) and Article VI, Section 28(1) (Progressive Taxation).**

---

### Ruling/s

#### 1. On Procedural Issues (Origination Clause, Bicameral Conference Committee Powers, Enrolled Bill Doctrine, Three-Reading Rule):
**NO.** R.A. No. 9337 does **NOT** violate Article VI, Section 24 or Article VI, Section 26(2) of the Constitution.

##### Rationale & Application:
* **Enrolled Bill Doctrine:** Citing *Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*, *Arroyo v. De Venecia*, and *Tolentino v. Secretary of Finance*, the signing of a bill by the Speaker of the House and Senate President and certification by House/Senate Secretaries are conclusive proof of its due enactment. Courts will not inquire into alleged non-observance of internal parliamentary rules.
* **Scope of Bicameral Conference Committee Authority:** The creation of a BCC is an intrinsic legislative mechanism under Art. VI, Sec. 16(3). The BCC has wide latitude to reconcile disagreeing provisions. Citing *Tolentino*, a conference committee may report an entirely new provision or delete agreed provisions, provided the changes are **germane** to the general subject matter referred to it (revenue generation and NIRC tax reform).
* **Germaneness of Changes:**
  * *Standby Authority:* Germane as a compromise bridging the Senate's 10% rate and the House's 12% rate.
  * *Deletion of No Pass-On Provisions:* Germane because the nature of VAT is an indirect consumption tax intended to be passed on to the end consumer. Deleting restrictions was well within the BCC's reconciliation powers.
  * *Non-VAT Income Tax Amendments:* The Senate's addition of corporate income tax amendments (SB 1950) was germane to the overall revenue-raising purpose initiated by HB 3555/3705, fully compliant with the Senate's power to "propose or concur with amendments" under Art. VI, Sec. 24.
* **Three-Reading Rule:** The requirement of three readings on separate days under Art. VI, Sec. 26(2) applies to the passage of a bill in each House prior to transmittal, NOT to the conference committee report reconciling differences. Requiring 3 readings on conference reports would lead to endless legislative deadlock.

#### 2. On Undue Delegation of Legislative Power (Standby Authority):
**NO.** Sections 4, 5, and 6 of R.A. No. 9337 do **NOT** constitute an undue delegation of legislative power.

##### Rationale & Application:
* **Delegation of Fact-Ascertainment vs. Power to Tax:** The power to tax belongs exclusively to Congress. However, Congress does not delegate legislative power when it enacts a law complete in itself and leaves its effective date or operational rate contingent upon the ascertainment of specified facts by an executive officer (*People v. Vera*, *Edu v. Ericta*).
* **Mandatory Character ("Shall"):** The proviso uses the mandatory word **"shall"** ("the President, upon recommendation of the Secretary of Finance, shall... raise the rate of value-added tax to 12%"). The President possesses **zero discretion** to refuse or modify the rate if the factual conditions (VAT/GDP > 2.8% or Deficit/GDP > 1.5%) are met.
* **Role of Secretary of Finance:** The Secretary of Finance merely performs a mathematical/administrative computation of GDP ratios and submits the factual finding. This is executive implementation, not lawmaking.

#### 3. On Substantive Due Process, Equal Protection, and Progressive Taxation:
**NO.** Section 8 and Section 12 do **NOT** violate Due Process, Equal Protection, or the rule on Progressive Taxation.

##### Rationale & Application:
* **Nature of Input VAT:** Input VAT credit is a statutory privilege created by the legislature under the tax credit method, not a vested property right immune from statutory regulation or limitation.
* **Rational Classification & Public Purpose:** Capping creditable input VAT at 70% and amortizing capital goods VAT over 60 months are reasonable policy measures to prevent tax evasion, plug revenue leakages, and ensure immediate cash flow to resuscitate an economy in distress.
* **Progressive Taxation:** The constitutional mandate for a progressive tax system (Art. VI, Sec. 28) is a flexible directive, not a judicial restriction disabling Congress from imposing indirect consumption taxes like VAT.

### Doctrines and Legal Basis

#### Constitutional Provisions (Verbatim):
* **Article VI, Section 16 (3), 1987 Constitution:**
  > "Each House may determine the rules of its proceedings..."
* **Article VI, Section 24, 1987 Constitution:**
  > "All appropriation, revenue or tariff bills, bills authorizing increase of the public debt, bills of local application, and private bills shall originate exclusively in the House of Representatives; but the Senate may propose or concur with amendments."
* **Article VI, Section 26 (2), 1987 Constitution:**
  > "No bill passed by either House shall become a law unless it has passed three readings on separate days, and printed copies thereof in its final form have been distributed to its Members three days before its passage, except when the President certifies to the necessity of its immediate enactment to meet a public calamity or emergency..."
* **Article VI, Section 28 (2), 1987 Constitution:**
  > "The Congress may, by law, authorize the President to fix within specified limits, and may impose, tariff rates, import and export quotas, tonnage and wharfage dues, and other duties or imposts within the framework of the national development program of the government."

#### Jurisprudence & Ratio Summary:
1. ***Tolentino v. Secretary of Finance*, 235 SCRA 630 (1994):** Settled that revenue *bills* (not statutes) must originate in the House; the Senate may rewrite or introduce germane amendments; Conference Committees have broad power to compromise germane provisions.
2. ***Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*, 463 Phil. 179 (2003):** Reaffirmed the Enrolled Bill Doctrine; signing and certification of a bill by legislative leaders are conclusive of due enactment.
3. ***People v. Vera*, 65 Phil. 56 (1937) & *Edu v. Ericta*, 35 SCRA 481 (1970):** Distinguishes non-delegable power to make laws from delegable authority or discretion as to its execution/fact-ascertainment.

### Summaries of Separate Opinions

#### 1. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Chief Justice Davide, Jr.:
* **Concurrence:** Concurs generally with the *ponencia* on VAT provisions.
* **Dissent on Origination Clause (Sec. 24):** Dissents regarding non-VAT NIRC amendments. Argues that Senate amendments and BCC insertions introducing corporate income tax, franchise tax, and excise tax amendments violated the House's exclusive constitutional authority to initiate non-VAT revenue measures.

#### 2. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Senior Associate Justice Puno:
* **Concurrence:** Concurs in the result.
* **Dissent on 3 Points:**
  1. *Prematurity:* Challenge to the "standby authority" is not ripe for adjudication.
  2. *Deletion of No Pass-On Provision:* Deleting the "no pass-on provision" on electricity violated the common intent of both Houses.
  3. *Section 21:* Section 21 (restrictions on LGU use of incremental VAT revenues) is an unconstitutional extraneous insertion.
* **Critique of BCC Powers:** Criticizes *Tolentino*'s broad "germaneness" test, warning that uncontrolled BCC powers turn conference committees into an unconstitutional "third house" exercising super veto powers.

#### 3. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Associate Justice Panganiban:
* **Concurrence:** Agrees that standby authority is valid (administrative fact-finding) and that the BCC could delete no pass-on provisions.
* **Dissent on Corporate Income Taxes:** Dissents on corporate income tax insertions, holding that adding non-VAT income tax amendments violated the Origination Clause and germaneness principle.
* **Enrolled Bill Limits:** Maintains that the Enrolled Bill Doctrine must yield to mandatory constitutional limitations.

#### 4. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Associate Justice Ynares-Santiago:
* **Dissent:** Dissents against untrammeled BCC powers. Holds that the BCC cannot substitute its judgment for Congress by deleting common provisions (no pass-on) or inserting unvoted amendments without undergoing 3 readings.

#### 5. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Associate Justice Sandoval-Gutierrez:
* **Dissent:** Dissents fully. Votes that standby authority is an unconstitutional delegation of legislative power ("no taxation without representation") and that non-VAT amendments violated the Origination Clause.

#### 6. Note on Associate Justice Corona:
* **Opinion:** Joins Mrs. Justice Sandoval-Gutierrez in her concurring and dissenting opinion.

#### 7. Note on Associate Justice Carpio-Morales:
* **Opinion:** Concurs with the majority, but joins Justice Tinga's dissent on Section 8.

#### 8. Note on Associate Justice Callejo, Sr.:
* **Opinion:** Joins Mr. Senior Justice Puno in his concurring and dissenting opinion.

#### 9. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Associate Justice Azcuna:
* **Concurrence:** Concurs with majority on VAT provisions and standby authority.
* **Dissent:** Dissents regarding non-VAT corporate income tax amendments (not germane to VAT).

#### 10. Concurring and Dissenting Opinion of Associate Justice Tinga:
* **Concurrence:** Concurs on validity of standby authority and BCC procedure.
* **Dissent on Section 8:** Dissents strongly on Section 8 (70% input VAT cap and capital goods amortization), arguing it violates substantive due process by mathematically destroying small and medium enterprises.

#### 11. Concurring Opinion of Associate Justice Chico-Nazario:
* **Concurrence:** Fully concurs with the *ponencia*. Reaffirms strong adherence to the Enrolled Bill Doctrine to prevent judicial chaos and respect coordinate branches.

#### 12. Note on Associate Justice Garcia:
* **Opinion:** Concurs with Justice Puno regarding deletion of the no pass-on provision and Section 21.
